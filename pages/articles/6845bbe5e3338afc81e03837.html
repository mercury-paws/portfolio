The Unfinished Question / 22.02.2025

A world made to be understood

What if the world is hard on purpose? Not broken, not indifferent — arranged. And what if, for all our struggling, we are genuinely unable to change the script we were handed?

It sounds like despair. Yes, but... What if a world built on purpose has a purpose, and a purpose can be reached? Whether we reach it depends entirely on how we learn. Maybe the difference is not in the event, but in the eye that meets it.

Our actions are already set. We move as we are moved, the way the planets turn, the way the tide answers the moon — precise, obedient, mechanical. Perhaps the world is closer to machinery: every choice the inevitable sum of everything that came before it and everything that follows. If that is true, then no one truly "does" anything. The deed was always going to happen.

Science has brushed up against this strangeness. In the 1980s, the neuroscientist Benjamin Libet found that the brain seems to begin an action a fraction of a second before we become aware of deciding to make it — as though the choice is already underway while we are still congratulating ourselves on making it. The experiment is argued over to this day, but the unease it leaves behind is hard to shake: perhaps the hand lifts, and only afterward does the self arrive to claim it.

And what if the deed was never the point?

The act is only the question on the page — the situation we are placed inside, scripted and unavoidable. What is being weighed is not what we did, but what rose in us while we did it. Did we notice? Did we understand? The ancient Egyptians imagined the heart laid on a scale against a single feather. They did not weigh the hands. They weighed the thing inside that knows.

The material falls away like snow on the sun, and what remains is quieter and harder to name: our awareness. The result seems to care only for the inner event: the recognition, the ache, the slow dawning of I see what this was. Every situation is an instrument, and we are made to feel its full range. Guilt, yes — but also grief, and awe, and fear, and tenderness, and more. We are not here to act well. We are here to feel accurately, and to know what we feel.

But a signal is not the destination. A person can feel guilt and change nothing, carry the ache and still walk the same path again tomorrow. That repetition is telling: guilt without understanding is only pain, a note struck over and over with no one listening. The feeling was supposed to lead somewhere. When it doesn't, the situation returns, wearing a different face, asking the same question again.

We are not obliged to call the wound a blessing. We are only asked to understand, to grasp why the thing happened and what it was.

The question the whole arrangement seems to pose, over and over, in a thousand costumes, is simply: did you understand?

And if you did — what then? Do we wait on the same step while most of us still stand with our eyes closed?

I'm not sure we're meant to know, yet.

A few of the old names for this idea:

Weighing of the heart — in ancient Egyptian belief, the heart of the dead was set on a scale against the feather of truth, judging the person not by their deeds but by what lay within them.

Metanoia — the Greek word used in early Christianity for repentance, meaning literally a change of mind, a turning of one's whole way of seeing rather than mere regret.

Dunya-as-test — in Islam, the earthly world (dunya) is understood as a temporary testing ground, where hardship and ease alike exist to reveal and refine the inner state.

Soul-school — a thread running through the dharmic traditions, where the cycle of worldly life functions as a school for consciousness, each experience teaching until understanding is complete.
